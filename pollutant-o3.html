Know Your Pollutants / O₃
Secondary pollutant

O₃
Ground-level ozone

Not emitted directly. Ozone forms when sunlight cooks other pollutants, and it peaks on hot, sunny afternoons.

What is it?

Ground-level ozone is created when NOx and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) react in sunlight. It is the main ingredient of photochemical smog. It is the same molecule as the protective stratospheric ozone layer, but harmful where we breathe it.

Where does it come from?

  • NOx from vehicles and industry
  • VOCs from fuel vapours, solvents and paints
  • Strong sunlight and warm, still weather
  • Transport of precursors from upwind areas

Health effects

  • Causes chest tightness, coughing and throat irritation
  • Reduces lung function and inflames airways
  • Aggravates asthma and bronchitis
  • Damages crops and vegetation

In Pune

Because ozone needs sunshine, it tends to peak in the afternoon and in the dry, sunny months, often away from the roads where the NOx originated.

Reduce your exposure

  • Exercise outdoors in the morning rather than the afternoon
  • Reduce fuel-vapour exposure when refuelling
  • Limit paint and solvent use in poorly ventilated rooms
  • Note: indoor air filters do not remove ozone unless they use activated carbon
