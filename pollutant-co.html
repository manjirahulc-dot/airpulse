Know Your Pollutants / CO
Invisible, odourless gas

CO
Carbon monoxide

A silent gas from incomplete combustion. It cannot be seen or smelled, yet it blocks oxygen delivery in your blood.

What is it?

When fuel burns without enough oxygen, carbon monoxide forms instead of CO₂. Inhaled CO binds to haemoglobin about 200 times more strongly than oxygen, starving organs of supply.

Where does it come from?

  • Petrol vehicles, especially in slow, idling traffic
  • Garbage and biomass burning
  • Cooking with wood, charcoal or kerosene
  • Generators and faulty heaters

Health effects

  • Low levels: headache, dizziness, fatigue
  • Higher levels: confusion, chest pain, loss of consciousness
  • Dangerous for people with heart disease
  • Indoor CO from enclosed combustion can be fatal

In Pune

Idling and stop-start traffic raise roadside CO. Headache (25) and fatigue (21) were common symptoms among survey respondents, though they have many possible causes.

Reduce your exposure

  • Never run engines or generators in closed spaces
  • Ensure proper ventilation for any cooking or heating fire
  • Install a CO alarm where fuel is burned indoors
  • Avoid idling, particularly in garages
