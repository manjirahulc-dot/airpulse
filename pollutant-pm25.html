Know Your Pollutants / PM2.5
Particles ≤ 2.5 µm

PM2.5
Fine particulate matter

Particles about 30 times thinner than a human hair, small enough to travel deep into the lungs and cross into the bloodstream.

What is it?

PM2.5 is a mix of solid and liquid droplets: soot, sulphates, nitrates, organic compounds and metals. Because of its tiny size it stays airborne for days and can travel hundreds of kilometres. Much of it is "secondary", formed in the air when gases like SO₂, NOx and ammonia react.

Where does it come from?

  • Vehicle exhaust, especially diesel
  • Burning of garbage, crop residue and biomass
  • Coal and industrial combustion
  • Cooking with solid fuels
  • Secondary formation from NOx, SO₂ and ammonia

Health effects

  • Penetrates deep into the alveoli and enters blood
  • Linked to asthma attacks, reduced lung function and bronchitis
  • Raises risk of heart attack, stroke and lung cancer with long-term exposure
  • Children, older adults and people with heart or lung disease are most affected

In Pune

Heavy traffic and vehicle smoke are among the most reported exposures in our survey (62 of 73 respondents), and tailpipe soot is a major PM2.5 contributor in city corridors. Winter inversions can trap it near the ground.

Reduce your exposure

  • Wear a well-fitted N95/FFP2 mask on high-AQI days; cloth and surgical masks filter little PM2.5
  • Avoid exercising beside busy roads
  • Use a HEPA air purifier indoors if possible
  • Check the AQI before outdoor activity
