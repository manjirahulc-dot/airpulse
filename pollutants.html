Pollutant guide

Know your pollutants.

The AQI is a single number, but it is built from several different pollutants, each with its own sources, health effects and ways to protect yourself. Pick one to learn more.

PM2.5Fine particulate matter

Particles about 30 times thinner than a human hair, small enough to travel deep into the lungs and cross into the bloodstream.

PM10Coarse particulate matter

Dust you can often see and taste. Larger than PM2.5, it mostly lodges in the nose, throat and upper airways.

NO₂Nitrogen dioxide

A sharp-smelling gas formed whenever fuel burns at high temperature. It is the clearest chemical fingerprint of traffic.

SO₂Sulphur dioxide

A gas released by burning sulphur-containing fuels, noticeable by its sharp, choking smell.

COCarbon monoxide

A silent gas from incomplete combustion. It cannot be seen or smelled, yet it blocks oxygen delivery in your blood.

O₃Ground-level ozone

Not emitted directly. Ozone forms when sunlight cooks other pollutants, and it peaks on hot, sunny afternoons.

How the AQI works

The AQI is calculated separately for each pollutant, and the reported value is the highest of them. That is why a day can be rated "Unhealthy" because of PM2.5 alone, even if every other pollutant is low.
