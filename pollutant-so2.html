Colourless, pungent gas

SO₂
Sulphur dioxide

A gas released by burning sulphur-containing fuels, noticeable by its sharp, choking smell.

What is it?

Sulphur dioxide forms when coal, oil and some biomass are burned. In the air it converts to sulphate particles, a major part of PM2.5, and to sulphuric acid that causes acid rain.

Where does it come from?

  • Coal-fired power plants
  • Industrial furnaces, refineries and smelters
  • Ships and heavy fuel oil
  • Burning of sulphur-rich waste

Health effects

  • Rapidly irritates eyes, nose, throat and airways
  • Can trigger bronchospasm within minutes in people with asthma
  • Worsens existing respiratory and heart conditions
  • Forms sulphate aerosols that add to PM2.5

In Pune

Indian fuel-sulphur standards have tightened over the years, so urban SO₂ is usually lower than PM or NO₂, but it matters near industrial areas (19 respondents reported industrial smoke).

Reduce your exposure

  • Avoid prolonged time near industrial emitters
  • Stay indoors during local pollution alerts
  • Asthma patients should keep reliever inhalers handy
  • Do not burn waste or low-grade fuels
